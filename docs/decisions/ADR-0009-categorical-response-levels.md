# ADR-0009: Categorical response levels instead of risk scores on screen

Status: accepted, amended 2026-11-17 and twice on 2026-11-18

## Context
The first version showed each bill's `billRiskScore`, a 0 to 100 weighted sum of stage progress, committee calendar, company position and issue impact, and raised a decision when the score rose by 10 points in a week. Two problems showed up. First, the number did not track the moment that mattered: in the sample week the sidewalk robot duties bill cleared the subcommittee and faced the full committee in three days, yet its score rose only from 74 to 78.8, because the calendar component was already full a week earlier. Second, a company executive reading "78.8" cannot tell which input moved, cannot argue with it, and reads precision into weights that were set by hand. GR judgment is a few named facts and a stance.

## Decision
On screen and in the weekly report, a bill carries one of four response levels, decided by explicit rules in `responseLevel` (`ontology/functions.ts`) and always shown with its reasons:

- **Act now:** it has a decisive step within 7 days (see the third amendment), the company wants it changed (oppose or amend), and either it moved this week or the company opposes it and the impact is high.
- **Engage:** the text is still open and the company wants it changed and it moved this week or has a decisive step or a first introduction within 30 days; or the impact is high and a decisive step or introduction is within 10 days, whatever the company's stance.
- **Monitor:** it moved this week, has a meeting within 30 days, the company wants it changed, or the impact is high.
- **Watch:** none of the above, or the bill is closed.

The assistant considers a decision only for a followed bill at Act now, and then names one person to persuade (see the decision rule in `agent/agent.ts`). Risks and opportunities are tagged the same way, with impact (high, medium) and urgency (imminent, in progress, ongoing) or a due date. `billRiskScore` stays in the schema with its formula published; it feeds `issueExposure` and is listed on the Ontology screen as a function.

## Known failure, and the amendment
The first rule required a stage change in the week. In the sample world that left Moru Foods' meat terms restriction bill at Engage: the company opposes it, the impact is high, and the bill review subcommittee takes it up in two days, but nothing had moved since it was placed on the agenda. Any analyst would call that urgent. A bill that sits on an agenda can still be voted on, and a move in the past week is a poor proxy for that. The rule now also treats an opposed, high-impact bill with a meeting within 7 days as Act now, without a move. An amend stance still needs a move, because a bill the company only wants adjusted is less likely to need a meeting this week if nothing has changed. Wrenfield Pay's local presence bill (opposed, high impact, full committee in three days) reaches Act now under the same clause.

Other gaps remain: a bill can be added to an agenda at a day's notice, after the weekly run; a meeting can be cancelled; and the rule knows nothing about how contested a bill is. The confirmation queue and the analyst's own reading have to cover these.

## Second amendment: notice periods and the reasons shown
The rules only looked at meetings. The government bill on sidewalk operation standards had no meeting scheduled, so the bill page said "No meeting scheduled in the next 30 days" while its comment period closed in three weeks and This week listed the same notice as a high-impact opportunity. For a government bill in legislative notice, the close of the notice period is the step that matters: it is the last easy moment to file comments. The ministry must consider them but is not bound, and the text can still change afterwards in regulatory review, the Ministry of Government Legislation review, the vice-ministers' and Cabinet meetings and the President's approval. That sequence takes months, so the sample world submits a government bill about two and a half months after its notice closes. A **step** is now either a committee, subcommittee or plenary meeting with the bill on the agenda, or the close of an open notice period, and the 7- and 30-day tests apply to the nearest step. Act now still needs a meeting for the assistant to raise a decision, because only a meeting has agenda-setters to persuade.

The bill page now shows the facts in two lists: **Grounds for this level** (facts that raise the level: a move, a step within 30 days, a stance that wants change, high impact) and **Factors against a higher level** (facts that hold it back). The sidewalk standards bill reads Monitor with the notice deadline under Grounds for this level and "no recorded position on this bill" under Factors against a higher level; once the analyst's request to amend is approved it reaches Engage.

## Third amendment: what a meeting can still change
A red team of a senior consultant and a prospective user found three places where the rules sent the reader to the wrong room.

- **A first 상정 is not a decision.** A full committee that takes up a bill that was only referred hears the proposer's statement and the expert review report and takes no vote; the text is written in the bill review subcommittee. The full committee counts as a decisive step only for a bill its subcommittee has passed. The subcommittee's review always counts. A first introduction still counts for Engage (the company should be working the subcommittee members before the bill gets there) but never for Act now, and never raises a decision. The Local presence bill therefore reaches Act now only once its subcommittee review is on the calendar, and the decision then names the subcommittee chair.
- **The Legislation and Judiciary Committee and the plenary do not change substance.** The Legislation and Judiciary Committee checks system and wording and can hold a bill back; the plenary votes on the text as it stands. A bill past the full committee stays at Monitor at most, and its reasons say that only wording and delay risk are left. The company's substantive ask moves to the stage that can change it: the subcommittee review of the related bill, or the committee before it adopts its alternative. The Robot insurance committee alternative is Monitor, and Aster's coverage ask sits on the weight class bill in the subcommittee. The assistant proposes a risk only for a bill whose text can still change.
- **Support does not hide an important bill.** A supported bill with high impact and a decisive step or introduction within 10 days is Engage. A Monitor label on a supported high-impact bill with its subcommittee review in 8 days read as inattention.

The decision target follows the same logic. It is chosen among the people who can change the text at the next decisive step, in this order: the chair of the bill review subcommittee (who writes it), the lead sponsor (a wording change the sponsor accepts is the easiest to carry), the committee chair (who sets the agenda and the vote). The other secretary only agrees the agenda and can delay, so is never picked. When the company opposes a bill the person's stance must be opposed or unconfirmed; when it wants amendments any stance qualifies, since a supporter can carry the wording. For Aster's sidewalk bill, after the subcommittee passed the stop rule, the committee chair and the subcommittee chair already have open plan steps, so the lead sponsor is the target. The alternative "accept the bill as it stands" now names the other routes (a committee supplementary resolution, 부대의견, or the enforcement decree) and the decision falls due two working days before the meeting, so a working day is left to act. It is never due before the working day after the as-of date; when the meeting is too close for both, it is due on the as-of date and the screen says Due today.

Bills on the same article are reviewed together in the subcommittee and leave it as one text, so the sample world moves them through together, and a bill that adds a cargo class or operation standards leaves them to the enforcement decree rather than the Act.

## Consequences
- Leadership sees the reason a bill is urgent in the words an analyst would use, and can dispute a specific fact.
- Escalation does not depend on tuning a threshold.
- Four levels lose ordering within a level; the screens sort by level, then by days to the next step.
- The rules are judgment written down. They are tested (`tests/functions.test.ts`, `tests/agent.test.ts`) but not fitted to outcomes, and a deployment should review them against real bill histories.
